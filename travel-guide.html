01 • DOCUMENTATION

Travel Planning & Official Visas

Pakistan operates an official electronic visa (e-Visa) portal. Citizens of over 175 countries can apply online, and tourists from 126+ nations qualify for simplified e-visa processing. Overseas Pakistanis travelling on a NICOP / POC card do not require a separate visa.

02 • MOBILITY

Transportation & Getting Around

International flights connect through Pakistan's major airports, including Islamabad, Karachi, and Lahore; check directly with airlines for current schedules. Domestic flights connect major cities to mountain hubs (Gilgit, Skardu, Chitral, Gwadar). Intercity travel is fast via the modern 6-lane National Motorway network (M-2, M-5). For northern valleys, hire licensed 4x4 vehicles for off-road passes.

Key Routes: M-2 Lahore-Islamabad • Karakoram Highway (N-35) • Makran Coastal Highway (N-10)
🏘
03 • LODGING

Accommodation & Stays

Options range from 5-star international city hotels in Lahore, Islamabad, and Karachi to restored royal palace havelis (Shigar Fort, Khaplu Palace), modern alpine glamping pods at Attabad Lake, and warm family-run valley homestays.

Tip: Book high-season northern accommodation (June–August) well in advance.
🤝
04 • ETIQUETTE

Local Customs & Etiquette

Pakistani culture is famed for legendary warmth and hospitality towards guests. Modest dress covering shoulders and knees is recommended. When entering mosques or Sufi shrines, shoes must be removed, and women should drape a light scarf over their heads.

Always request permission before photographing local individuals, particularly in rural villages.
🏕
05 • HEALTH & SAFETY

Safety & High-Altitude Guidance

When travelling to elevations over 2,500m (Hunza, Skardu, Deosai), allow at least 24 hours to acclimatise before strenuous treks. Drink plenty of water to prevent Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). Drink bottled or filtered water, and pack comprehensive travel medical insurance.

Emergency Contact: Tourist Police Help & Emergency 15 • Rescue 1122
🌡
06 • CLIMATE

Seasonal Packing & Considerations

Pakistan's seasons vary dramatically by altitude: summers in the southern plains (Sindh and Punjab) are hot, making northern valleys the natural haven. Conversely, winters are ideal for heritage exploration in Lahore, Multan, and Mohenjo-daro, or coastal drives along the Makran Coast.

Layering: High mountain nights can freeze even in mid-July. Always pack windproof jackets.

Questions Before You Travel

Practical clarifications to common queries from international and domestic visitors.

Citizens of over 175 countries can easily obtain an official electronic visa online via the government's official portal (visa.nadra.gov.pk). Overseas Pakistanis possessing a valid National Identity Card for Overseas Pakistanis (NICOP) or Pakistan Origin Card (POC) can enter Pakistan without a visa.
May through October is the premier window for Gilgit-Baltistan, Hunza, and Skardu. April offers magnificent spring apricot blossoms across the valleys, while October is renowned for vibrant golden autumn foliage. High mountain passes like Babusar Pass and Deosai Plateau open only between late June and October.
Between Lahore, Islamabad, and Multan, the 6-lane modern motorways (M-2 and M-5) provide smooth driving in 3.5 to 4 hours. Direct domestic flights connect Islamabad, Karachi, and Lahore to northern airports (Gilgit and Skardu). Modern luxury air-conditioned bus services (such as Daewoo and Faisal Movers) also connect all major cities.
Yes. English is an official language of Pakistan, widely spoken and understood across hotels, airports, restaurants, transport hubs, and by tour guides. Road signs, menus, and official information are routinely printed in both Urdu and English.
The currency is the Pakistani Rupee (PKR). Major international credit and debit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are accepted in hotels, upscale restaurants, and major stores in cities.
